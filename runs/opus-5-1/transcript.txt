## What I found

The existing suite caught one bug (`discontinue` setting `"active"`). I reproduced eight more against the original code before fixing anything — every one of my adversarial probes "passed", which was itself the finding:

| Probe | Original behaviour |
|---|---|
| Multi-line reserve, 2nd line short | threw, but **kept the 1st line** — 10 units lost with no order to cancel |
| `qty: -50` | **minted stock**: 10 on hand → availability 60 |
| `qty: NaN` | passed the `>` check; availability `NaN` **permanently** |
| Same `orderId` twice | double-booked — not retry-safe |

## Judgment calls worth your attention

**Cancel into a discontinued SKU is refused** — and since there's no `reactivate`, that stock is stranded with no recovery path. I chose this over silently resurrecting sellable stock for a withdrawn product, but it's a real gap needing an operational answer, not something I could close here. It's §2.1 in the report, flagged as the most important limitation.

**Rounding is half-up on the discount amount.** That's a business decision I made rather than one I was given; confirm it with whoever owns pricing.

**Seven intentional breaking changes**, all converting silent corruption into loud failure — the notable one being `Catalog.get()` returning a frozen copy, so any caller mutating that result must switch to `discontinue()`. All enumerated in the report.

The two biggest remaining risks are **not covered by tests and not fixable inside this module**: there's no concurrency control (`reserve`'s check-then-commit is atomic only because it's synchronous — one `await` reopens the oversell window), and no durability at all. I'd treat both as blockers before this backs real stock.

Two things I'd flag about my own process: I burned a couple of minutes when `vitest --root /` started scanning your filesystem, and one of my new tests asserted availability 96 where the ledger arithmetic gives 97 — I predicted that failure before running, and it was my expectation that was wrong, not the code.
