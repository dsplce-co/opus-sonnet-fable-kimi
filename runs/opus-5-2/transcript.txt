## What I found

Baseline was 1/5 failing. I reproduced every defect with a failing assertion before fixing it — ten beyond the one the suite caught:

**Data-integrity, worst first:**
- **`reserve` was not atomic** — it checked and pushed line-by-line, so a failure on line 2 left line 1 permanently committed with no order record to undo it. Silent stock loss.
- **Callers could rewrite internal state** — `Catalog.get()` and `getMovements()` returned live references. Setting `sku.initial` drove availability to 999,899.
- **Negative qty minted stock** (`-(-5)` = a positive movement); **`NaN` qty** slipped past `NaN > available` and poisoned availability permanently.
- **`reserve` wasn't idempotent** — a client retry double-reserved (100 → 40 instead of 70).
- **`Catalog.add` silently overwrote** an existing SKU, resetting the `initial` anchor under existing movements.
- **`discontinue` set state to `"active"`** — the failing test, and it made every lifecycle rule unenforceable.
- **Discontinued SKUs were freely reservable.**
- **`lineValue` returned fractional money** (`2697.3000000000002` in a field documented as integer minor units) and accepted `-100%` (200% of gross) or `500%` (a credit).

## Design calls worth your attention

**`cancel` accepts a discontinued SKU even though `reserve` refuses one.** Deliberate asymmetry: refusing the return would pin that order's units in the reserved state forever — the exact stock loss the module exists to prevent. `discontinued` means "no new reservations", not "this stock ceases to exist."

**Six deliberate breaking changes** are listed explicitly in REPORT.md §1 (duplicate `add` throws, frozen returns, discontinued SKUs rejected, one movement per distinct SKU rather than per line, `getMovements` copies, `lineValue` rounds and validates). Each trades a silent integrity failure for a loud error. They belong in a release note.

## The gap I could not close

This is in the report, but it's the thing I'd raise before anyone wires it to real stock: **there is no concurrency control beyond the JS event loop.** `reserve`'s check-then-commit is atomic only *within* one synchronous call. Two processes, or one process with a database behind it, will still race. The in-process idempotency ledger is not a substitute for a transaction plus a uniqueness constraint on `orderId`. Related: nothing is persisted, so a restart silently resets availability to `initial`.
