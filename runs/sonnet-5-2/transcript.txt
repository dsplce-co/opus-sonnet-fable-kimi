Done. Summary of what I found and fixed:

**Bugs fixed:**
1. `catalog.discontinue()` set state to `"active"` instead of `"discontinued"` — the one test already catching this.
2. `Catalog.add()` had no validation — silently overwrote duplicate SKU ids, accepted negative/fractional quantities and prices.
3. `Inventory.reserve()` accepted negative `qty`, which inverted sign into a *positive* movement — an adversarial call could manufacture stock from nothing.
4. `reserve()` ignored SKU lifecycle — could reserve stock for discontinued SKUs.
5. `reserve()` wasn't atomic across a multi-line order — a failing line left earlier lines' movements committed.
6. `reserve()` had no idempotency — retrying the same order call double-reserved stock.
7. `getMovements()` returned the live internal array — callers could corrupt inventory history directly.
8. `OrderService.lineValue()` used float arithmetic on money, producing non-integer minor units, and had no bounds on qty/discount.

**Added:**
- `cancel(orderId)` — atomic, idempotent, respects reserved→cancelled lifecycle.
- `history(sku, fromSeq, toSeq)` — inclusive-range movement lookup.

Every change was verified incrementally against `npx vitest run` (predicted outcome first, then compared), plus a 12-case scratch test file covering the new/adversarial paths (removed before finishing, per your instructions — nothing left behind but the module itself). Final state: all 5 original tests pass, `tsc --noEmit` is clean.

Full writeup is in `REPORT.md`, including the production-readiness review of what I deliberately did *not* change (no persistence layer, no cross-process concurrency control, O(n) `available()` scans) and the three breaking changes worth flagging (duplicate-SKU `add()` now throws, `reserve()` now rejects invalid/discontinued/conflicting-retry input, `getMovements()` returns a copy).
